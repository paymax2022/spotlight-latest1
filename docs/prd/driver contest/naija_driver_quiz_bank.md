# Naija Driver — 3-Stage Safe Driving Assessment

**Quiz bank v1.0.0 · 90 questions · 120 seconds per question · Nigerian (FRSC) context**

Each stage contains 30 multiple-choice questions. The correct answer and a one-line coaching explanation follow each question (hide these in contestant view; surface the explanation after the answer is locked in, as a learning moment).

## Stage 1: Foundation — Road Rules, Signs & Documentation

*Basic knowledge of Nigerian road signs, traffic rules, required documents and rights of way.*  
*30 questions · 120s each · suggested pass mark: 70%*

**Q01. In Nigeria, what shape are most warning signs (e.g. 'bend ahead', 'narrow bridge')?**
- A) Rectangular
- B) Triangular
- C) Circular
- D) Octagonal

> **Answer: B** — Triangular signs warn of hazards ahead. Circular signs give orders; rectangular signs give information.

**Q02. A circular road sign with a red border generally means:**
- A) Information for drivers
- B) A warning of danger ahead
- C) Parking is permitted
- D) Prohibition — something is not allowed

> **Answer: D** — Red-bordered circles are prohibitive/regulatory signs, e.g. 'No entry', 'No overtaking'.

**Q03. What must you do at a red traffic light?**
- A) Stop only if pedestrians are crossing
- B) Slow down and proceed if clear
- C) Sound your horn and proceed
- D) Stop completely behind the stop line

> **Answer: D** — Red means a complete stop behind the stop line until the light turns green.

**Q04. A flashing amber (yellow) traffic light means:**
- A) Stop and wait for green
- B) Speed up before it turns red
- C) The light is broken — ignore it
- D) Proceed with caution, giving way where required

> **Answer: D** — Flashing amber allows you to proceed carefully while giving way to pedestrians and other traffic as required.

**Q05. Which agency is primarily responsible for road traffic safety and driver's licensing in Nigeria?**
- A) NAFDAC
- B) FRSC (Federal Road Safety Corps)
- C) NDLEA
- D) EFCC

> **Answer: B** — The FRSC administers road safety, driver's licences and highway regulations in Nigeria.

**Q06. What is the FRSC national toll-free emergency number?**
- A) 767
- B) 122
- C) 199
- D) 911

> **Answer: B** — Dial 122 (toll-free) to reach the FRSC in a road emergency or crash.

**Q07. The minimum age to obtain a driver's licence in Nigeria is:**
- A) 16 years
- B) 18 years
- C) 17 years
- D) 21 years

> **Answer: B** — You must be at least 18 years old to hold a Nigerian driver's licence.

**Q08. In Nigeria, vehicles are driven on which side of the road?**
- A) Either side
- B) The left side
- C) The right side
- D) The centre of the road

> **Answer: C** — Nigeria uses right-hand traffic: keep right, and overtaking is normally done on the left.

**Q09. A zebra crossing is designed for:**
- A) Cyclists to ride across
- B) Vehicles to overtake
- C) Animals to cross
- D) Pedestrians to cross the road safely

> **Answer: D** — Zebra crossings give pedestrians priority; drivers must slow down and stop for people on the crossing.

**Q10. A red octagonal (eight-sided) sign always means:**
- A) STOP
- B) No parking
- C) Give way
- D) Danger ahead

> **Answer: A** — The octagon is reserved for STOP: come to a complete halt before proceeding.

**Q11. Which of these is NOT a document you are required to have for your vehicle?**
- A) Driver's licence
- B) International passport
- C) Certificate of roadworthiness
- D) Vehicle insurance certificate

> **Answer: B** — Required papers include your licence, insurance, roadworthiness and proof of ownership — not a passport.

**Q12. A standard Nigerian driver's licence is issued with a validity of:**
- A) 3 or 5 years
- B) 10 years
- C) 1 year
- D) 2 years

> **Answer: A** — FRSC issues driver's licences with 3-year and 5-year validity options.

**Q13. A triangular sign with an exclamation mark (!) means:**
- A) End of speed limit
- B) Road permanently closed
- C) General danger/hazard ahead
- D) Filling station ahead

> **Answer: C** — The exclamation triangle is a general warning: slow down and be prepared for a hazard.

**Q14. A green traffic light means:**
- A) Go immediately regardless of traffic
- B) Proceed only if the junction is clear and safe
- C) Pedestrians have right of way over you in all directions
- D) Prepare to stop

> **Answer: B** — Green permits you to go, but only when your exit and the junction are clear and it is safe.

**Q15. A road sign showing two children walking usually warns of:**
- A) No minors allowed on this road
- B) A school or children crossing ahead
- C) A playground for adults
- D) A toy market ahead

> **Answer: B** — Slow down near school zones — children may cross suddenly.

**Q16. Rectangular road signs are generally used to:**
- A) Provide information or direction
- B) Give compulsory orders
- C) Indicate speed limits only
- D) Warn of danger

> **Answer: A** — Rectangles are informative signs: directions, distances, facilities and services.

**Q17. Every vehicle in Nigeria must display:**
- A) Number plates at the front and rear
- B) A tinted-glass permit
- C) The owner's photograph
- D) A national flag

> **Answer: A** — Valid number plates must be fixed and clearly visible at the front and rear of the vehicle.

**Q18. When the traffic light changes from green to amber (yellow), you should:**
- A) Sound your horn and continue
- B) Reverse away from the junction
- C) Prepare to stop safely before the line
- D) Accelerate to beat the red

> **Answer: C** — Amber means stop if you can do so safely — never accelerate to 'beat' the light.

**Q19. A continuous (unbroken) white line in the centre of the road means:**
- A) It marks a bus lane
- B) It marks a parking zone
- C) Overtaking is allowed at any time
- D) Do not cross or overtake across the line

> **Answer: D** — A solid centre line prohibits crossing or overtaking because visibility or conditions are unsafe.

**Q20. A broken white centre line means:**
- A) The road ends ahead
- B) No overtaking at any time
- C) You may overtake when it is safe to do so
- D) It is a one-way street

> **Answer: C** — Broken lines allow crossing/overtaking, but only when you can see it is completely safe.

**Q21. The general speed limit for cars in built-up areas (towns and cities) in Nigeria is:**
- A) 50 km/h
- B) 100 km/h
- C) 30 km/h
- D) 80 km/h

> **Answer: A** — In built-up areas the limit for cars is 50 km/h due to pedestrians, junctions and heavy activity.

**Q22. Before starting to drive, the first things to adjust are your:**
- A) Number plates
- B) Seat and mirrors
- C) Tyres
- D) Radio and air-conditioner

> **Answer: B** — A correct seating position and properly set mirrors are essential for control and visibility.

**Q23. Seat belts must be worn by:**
- A) The driver only
- B) Only on expressways
- C) The driver and all passengers
- D) Front-seat occupants only

> **Answer: C** — Nigerian law requires the driver and all passengers to use seat belts on every trip.

**Q24. The 'No Entry' sign is best described as:**
- A) A yellow triangle
- B) A blue square with an arrow
- C) A red circle with a white horizontal bar
- D) A green circle

> **Answer: C** — The red disc with a white bar means the road is closed to traffic in that direction.

**Q25. Where there is no footpath, pedestrians should walk:**
- A) In the middle of the road
- B) With their back to traffic
- C) Only at night
- D) Facing oncoming traffic

> **Answer: D** — Facing traffic lets pedestrians see approaching vehicles and step aside in time.

**Q26. Sounding your horn near a hospital or in a marked 'silence zone' is:**
- A) Allowed only at night
- B) Encouraged
- C) Compulsory
- D) Prohibited

> **Answer: D** — Horns are banned in silence zones such as hospital areas; the sign shows a crossed-out horn.

**Q27. Third-party motor insurance covers:**
- A) Theft of your own car
- B) All damage to your own car
- C) Injury or damage you cause to other people and their property
- D) Your fuel costs

> **Answer: C** — Third-party cover is the legal minimum: it protects others against loss you cause, not your own vehicle.

**Q28. A certificate of roadworthiness confirms that:**
- A) The owner has paid income tax
- B) The vehicle is mechanically fit to be on the road
- C) The car has been washed
- D) The driver can drive well

> **Answer: B** — Roadworthiness certifies the vehicle's brakes, lights, tyres and body are in safe condition.

**Q29. When approaching a junction with no signs or traffic lights, you should:**
- A) Slow down, look both ways and be ready to give way
- B) Speed through quickly before others arrive
- C) Flash your headlights and continue
- D) Sound your horn and continue at speed

> **Answer: A** — Uncontrolled junctions demand reduced speed and readiness to stop for crossing traffic.

**Q30. A learner driver in Nigeria must:**
- A) Display 'L' plates and be accompanied by a licensed driver
- B) Avoid all main roads permanently
- C) Drive only at night
- D) Drive only automatic cars

> **Answer: A** — Learners must show L plates and drive under the supervision of a fully licensed driver.

## Stage 2: Intermediate — Safe Driving Practice & FRSC Regulations

*Practical safe-driving behaviour: following distance, overtaking, speed limits, lane discipline and vehicle regulations.*  
*30 questions · 120s each · suggested pass mark: 75%*

**Q01. The 'two-second rule' is used to:**
- A) Time traffic lights
- B) Keep a safe following distance from the vehicle ahead
- C) Calculate fuel consumption
- D) Measure engine speed

> **Answer: B** — Pick a fixed point; if you pass it less than two seconds after the car ahead, you are too close.

**Q02. On wet roads or in rain, your following distance should be:**
- A) The same as in dry weather
- B) Ignored because of the wipers
- C) Reduced to stay in convoy
- D) At least doubled — about four seconds

> **Answer: D** — Wet roads can double stopping distances, so double your gap to at least four seconds.

**Q03. In Nigeria (right-hand traffic), you should normally overtake other vehicles on the:**
- A) Left side
- B) Either side freely
- C) Right side
- D) Road shoulder

> **Answer: A** — With traffic keeping right, overtaking is done on the left, returning right when safely clear.

**Q04. It is most dangerous to overtake:**
- A) During daylight
- B) At bends, hilltops, junctions and pedestrian crossings
- C) On a dual carriageway
- D) On a straight, clear road

> **Answer: B** — Never overtake where your view of oncoming traffic or crossing hazards is blocked.

**Q05. The maximum speed limit for private cars on Nigerian expressways is:**
- A) 140 km/h
- B) 100 km/h
- C) 80 km/h
- D) 120 km/h

> **Answer: B** — Cars are limited to 100 km/h on expressways (80 km/h on other highways, 50 km/h in town).

**Q06. The legal blood alcohol concentration (BAC) limit for drivers in Nigeria is:**
- A) 0.00% — but only for learners
- B) 0.50%
- C) 0.10%
- D) 0.05%

> **Answer: D** — FRSC enforces a 0.05 g/100ml BAC limit; the safest choice is not to drink at all before driving.

**Q07. Using a handheld mobile phone while driving is:**
- A) Allowed for business calls
- B) A punishable traffic offence
- C) Allowed if the call is short
- D) Allowed in slow traffic

> **Answer: B** — Phone use while driving is an FRSC offence — it multiplies your crash risk. Park safely to make calls.

**Q08. FRSC requires commercial vehicles in Nigeria to be fitted with:**
- A) A speed limiting device
- B) A television screen
- C) External loudspeakers
- D) Extra roof seats

> **Answer: A** — Speed limiters are mandatory on commercial vehicles to curb overspeeding, the top cause of crashes.

**Q09. The minimum legal tyre tread depth is generally:**
- A) 1.6 mm
- B) 0.5 mm
- C) 10 mm
- D) 5 mm

> **Answer: A** — Below 1.6 mm of tread, grip in the wet collapses and the tyre is illegal — check tyres weekly.

**Q10. When another vehicle is overtaking you, you should:**
- A) Brake suddenly
- B) Swerve towards it to warn the driver
- C) Accelerate so it cannot pass
- D) Maintain or slightly reduce your speed and keep right

> **Answer: D** — Help the overtaker pass safely; speeding up prolongs the manoeuvre and creates danger.

**Q11. The correct routine before changing lanes is:**
- A) Signal only, then move
- B) Horn, then swerve
- C) Mirror — Signal — check blind spot — then Manoeuvre
- D) Brake, turn, then signal

> **Answer: C** — The MSM routine with a blind-spot (shoulder) check prevents side-swipe collisions.

**Q12. You should signal your intention to turn:**
- A) In good time — at least about 30 metres before the turn
- B) At the exact moment of turning
- C) Only when other cars are visible
- D) After completing the turn

> **Answer: A** — Early, clear signals give other road users time to react to your intentions.

**Q13. Leaving a broken-down vehicle on the road at night without lights or reflectors is:**
- A) Allowed on expressways only
- B) A serious hazard and an offence
- C) Normal practice on quiet roads
- D) Acceptable if you will return soon

> **Answer: B** — Unlit stationary vehicles cause fatal night crashes — use hazard lights and place your warning triangle.

**Q14. 'Tailgating' means:**
- A) Following the vehicle ahead too closely
- B) Reversing on a highway
- C) Overtaking on the shoulder
- D) Driving with the boot open

> **Answer: A** — Tailgating removes your stopping margin; if the car ahead brakes hard, you will hit it.

**Q15. At a zebra crossing, the right of way belongs to:**
- A) Pedestrians on the crossing
- B) Commercial buses
- C) Motorcycles
- D) The fastest approaching vehicle

> **Answer: A** — Slow down on approach and stop for anyone on or stepping onto the crossing.

**Q16. In heavy rain during the day, you should:**
- A) Use hazard lights while moving
- B) Use full beam headlights
- C) Switch on dipped (low beam) headlights
- D) Drive with no lights to save the battery

> **Answer: C** — Dipped headlights make you visible in rain and spray; hazard lights while moving confuse other drivers.

**Q17. If dazzled by the high beams of an oncoming vehicle at night:**
- A) Stop suddenly in your lane
- B) Slow down and look towards the right edge of the road
- C) Close your eyes briefly until it passes
- D) Flash your lights repeatedly and speed up

> **Answer: B** — Using the road edge as a guide keeps you on course until your vision recovers.

**Q18. Overloading a vehicle beyond its rated capacity:**
- A) Reduces braking efficiency and stability, and is an offence
- B) Is allowed for short trips
- C) Saves fuel
- D) Improves road grip

> **Answer: A** — Excess load lengthens braking distance, strains tyres and destabilises the vehicle.

**Q19. Young children travelling in a car should:**
- A) Sit on the driver's lap
- B) Sit in front without a belt
- C) Stand between the front seats
- D) Sit in the back seat, properly restrained

> **Answer: D** — The back seat with an appropriate restraint is the safest position for children.

**Q20. When entering a roundabout, you should give way to:**
- A) Only trucks and tankers
- B) Nobody — first come, first served
- C) Vehicles behind you
- D) Traffic already circulating in the roundabout

> **Answer: D** — Vehicles already in the roundabout have priority; enter only when there is a safe gap.

**Q21. Hazard warning lights should be used:**
- A) When your vehicle is stationary and causing an obstruction, or in an emergency
- B) Whenever you overtake
- C) While driving normally in light rain
- D) To thank other drivers

> **Answer: A** — Hazard lights warn of danger or breakdown — using them while moving normally misleads other drivers.

**Q22. 'Defensive driving' means:**
- A) Fitting protective bars to your vehicle
- B) Driving very slowly at all times
- C) Anticipating hazards and the mistakes of other road users
- D) Driving aggressively to defend your lane

> **Answer: C** — A defensive driver expects errors from others and always leaves room and time to respond.

**Q23. If you begin to feel sleepy while driving, the safest action is to:**
- A) Stop in a safe place and rest
- B) Drive faster to finish the trip quickly
- C) Turn up the music loudly
- D) Open the window and continue

> **Answer: A** — No trick beats sleep. A short nap in a safe place can save your life — fatigue kills.

**Q24. On long journeys, FRSC advises drivers to take a rest break:**
- A) Every ten hours
- B) About every two hours, or whenever tired
- C) Never — breaks waste time
- D) Only when fuel runs out

> **Answer: B** — Regular breaks keep reaction time sharp; fatigue builds up silently on long trips.

**Q25. When passing motorcyclists (okada) and cyclists, you should:**
- A) Force them onto the shoulder
- B) Pass as closely as possible
- C) Give them extra clearance and reduce speed
- D) Sound your horn continuously

> **Answer: C** — Two-wheelers can wobble or swerve around potholes — leave generous side space.

**Q26. Compared with private cars, tankers and trailers are subject to:**
- A) Higher speed limits
- B) Exactly the same limits
- C) No speed limits
- D) Lower speed limits

> **Answer: D** — Heavy vehicles need far longer to stop, so their limits (e.g. 45–60 km/h) are lower than cars'.

**Q27. The recommended way to hold the steering wheel is:**
- A) Steering with the knees on straight roads
- B) One hand resting at the bottom
- C) Both hands on the wheel, about the 9 and 3 o'clock position
- D) One finger on the rim

> **Answer: C** — Two hands at 9 and 3 give maximum control for emergency steering.

**Q28. When joining an expressway from a slip road (acceleration lane), you should:**
- A) Reverse if no gap appears
- B) Stop at the end of the lane and wait
- C) Merge immediately at very low speed
- D) Build up speed to match traffic and merge when there is a safe gap

> **Answer: D** — Matching traffic speed lets you merge smoothly without forcing others to brake.

**Q29. Wearing a seat belt in a crash roughly:**
- A) Only helps below 30 km/h
- B) Halves your risk of death or serious injury
- C) Doubles your risk of injury
- D) Makes no measurable difference

> **Answer: B** — Belts cut the risk of death for front-seat occupants by about 45–50% — buckle up every trip.

**Q30. At a railway level crossing without barriers or lights, you should:**
- A) Stop, look both ways and listen before crossing
- B) Drive across as quickly as possible
- C) Follow the vehicle in front without checking
- D) Sound your horn and cross

> **Answer: A** — Trains cannot stop for you. Cross only after confirming the line is completely clear.

## Stage 3: Advanced — Hazard Perception & Emergency Response

*Defensive driving judgement: emergencies, skids, night/weather driving, crash-scene response and risk anticipation.*  
*30 questions · 120s each · suggested pass mark: 80%*

**Q01. If your brakes fail while driving, you should first:**
- A) Accelerate to escape the traffic around you
- B) Switch off the engine and remove the key
- C) Jump out of the moving vehicle
- D) Pump the brake pedal, shift to a lower gear and apply the handbrake gradually

> **Answer: D** — Pumping may restore pressure; engine braking and gradual handbrake use scrub off speed under control.

**Q02. If a tyre bursts at speed, you should:**
- A) Grip the steering firmly, ease off the accelerator and slow down gradually
- B) Swerve quickly to the shoulder at speed
- C) Switch off the ignition at once
- D) Brake as hard as possible immediately

> **Answer: A** — Hard braking or sharp steering after a blowout causes loss of control — decelerate smoothly first.

**Q03. 'Aquaplaning' (hydroplaning) happens when:**
- A) Tyres lose contact with the road on a film of water
- B) The road surface is dusty
- C) The wipers stop working
- D) The engine floods with fuel

> **Answer: A** — At speed on standing water, tyres ride on the water film and steering and braking stop working.

**Q04. If your car begins to aquaplane, you should:**
- A) Brake as hard as possible
- B) Ease off the accelerator, keep the steering straight and avoid braking suddenly
- C) Accelerate through the water
- D) Turn the wheel sharply to regain grip

> **Answer: B** — Lifting off gently lets the tyres slow and regain contact; sudden inputs cause a skid.

**Q05. If your vehicle catches fire while driving:**
- A) Keep driving to the nearest mechanic
- B) Continue slowly with the windows open
- C) Open the bonnet fully to inspect the flames
- D) Stop safely, switch off the ignition, get everyone out and away, then tackle only a small fire with an extinguisher

> **Answer: D** — Life first: evacuate upwind and call for help. Never fully open a burning bonnet — it feeds the fire air.

**Q06. On a highway, the warning triangle (C-caution) should be placed approximately:**
- A) 200 metres ahead of the vehicle
- B) 45 metres behind the vehicle
- C) 5 metres behind the vehicle
- D) On the roof of the vehicle

> **Answer: B** — About 45 m gives approaching traffic time to see and react before reaching your vehicle.

**Q07. To correct a rear-wheel skid, you should:**
- A) Accelerate hard out of it
- B) Pull the handbrake firmly
- C) Ease off the pedals and steer gently in the direction the rear is sliding
- D) Brake hard and steer away from the skid

> **Answer: C** — Steering into the skid realigns the car; harsh braking or power makes the slide worse.

**Q08. The most important rule for night driving is:**
- A) Follow the tail lights ahead closely
- B) Use hazard lights while moving
- C) Use full beam at all times
- D) Never drive faster than you can stop within the distance your headlights show

> **Answer: D** — If a hazard appears at the edge of your beam, you must be able to stop before reaching it.

**Q09. When meeting an oncoming vehicle at night, you should:**
- A) Flash your lights repeatedly
- B) Switch off your lights completely
- C) Dip your headlights to low beam
- D) Keep full beam on for better vision

> **Answer: C** — Dipping prevents dazzling the other driver — a dazzled driver may drift into your lane.

**Q10. During harmattan haze or fog, you should:**
- A) Drive at normal speed to exit the fog quickly
- B) Use full beam to see farther
- C) Use dipped headlights or fog lights, reduce speed and increase following distance
- D) Use only parking lights

> **Answer: C** — Full beam reflects off haze and blinds you; slow down and extend your gap — visibility can drop suddenly.

**Q11. Arriving first at a crash scene, your first action should be to:**
- A) Start moving all victims immediately
- B) Take photographs before anything else
- C) Drive past quickly to avoid delay
- D) Park safely, switch on hazard lights, protect the scene and call 122

> **Answer: D** — Securing the scene prevents a second crash; then summon FRSC/emergency services on 122.

**Q12. A crash victim with a suspected neck or spinal injury should be:**
- A) Carried on someone's back to a car
- B) Given water to drink
- C) Left in position and not moved unless there is immediate danger such as fire
- D) Sat upright immediately to help breathing

> **Answer: C** — Movement can turn a spinal injury into permanent paralysis — wait for trained responders unless danger forces evacuation.

**Q13. Total stopping distance is made up of:**
- A) Engine size plus tyre width
- B) Speed minus reaction time
- C) Braking distance only
- D) Thinking (reaction) distance plus braking distance

> **Answer: D** — You travel a long way while reacting before the brakes even begin to work.

**Q14. If you double your speed, your braking distance becomes roughly:**
- A) Half as long
- B) Twice as long
- C) Exactly the same
- D) Four times as long

> **Answer: D** — Braking distance rises with the square of speed — small speed increases cost big stopping distance.

**Q15. If the engine overheats, you should:**
- A) Open the radiator cap immediately to release pressure
- B) Pull over safely, switch off the engine and wait for it to cool before checking
- C) Pour cold water on the hot engine block
- D) Keep driving to burn off the heat

> **Answer: B** — A hot radiator cap can erupt with scalding steam; cold water on a hot block can crack it.

**Q16. Sleeping inside a parked car with the engine and air-conditioner running is dangerous mainly because of:**
- A) Carbon monoxide poisoning
- B) Fuel gauge damage
- C) Tyre wear
- D) Battery drain

> **Answer: A** — Exhaust fumes can seep into the cabin; carbon monoxide is odourless and can kill sleeping occupants.

**Q17. A 'black spot' on a road refers to:**
- A) A location with a history of frequent crashes
- B) An oil stain on the tarmac
- C) A speed camera position
- D) A toll gate

> **Answer: A** — Approach marked black spots with extra caution and reduced speed.

**Q18. Strong crosswinds on bridges and exposed roads most affect:**
- A) High-sided vehicles, motorcycles — and any vehicle's steering, so grip the wheel firmly
- B) Only small-engine cars
- C) Only parked vehicles
- D) Nothing on a modern car

> **Answer: A** — Sudden gusts can push you off line, especially when passing trucks or gaps in buildings.

**Q19. A long articulated truck ahead signals and swings wide before a junction. You should:**
- A) Flash your lights to hurry it up
- B) Sound your horn and squeeze past
- C) Hold back — it needs extra room to complete its turn
- D) Quickly overtake through the gap it has opened

> **Answer: C** — Long vehicles swing out before turning; the 'gap' will close on anything beside them.

**Q20. A defensive driver always maintains a 'space cushion', meaning:**
- A) Extra passengers for balance
- B) A fully loaded boot
- C) Soft padded seats
- D) Space around the vehicle that allows an escape route if something goes wrong

> **Answer: D** — Managing the space ahead, behind and beside you is the core of defensive driving.

**Q21. In an emergency stop with ABS brakes, you should:**
- A) Pump the pedal rapidly
- B) Release the brake when the pedal vibrates
- C) Press the brake firmly, keep it pressed, and steer around the hazard if needed
- D) Use only the handbrake

> **Answer: C** — The pulsing pedal is ABS working — maintain firm pressure; ABS lets you steer while braking.

**Q22. When descending a long, steep hill, you should:**
- A) Ride the brakes continuously all the way down
- B) Switch off the engine
- C) Shift to neutral to save fuel
- D) Engage a lower gear and use engine braking

> **Answer: D** — Engine braking controls speed without overheating the brakes; neutral or engine-off removes control.

**Q23. 'Brake fade' is caused by:**
- A) Overheating of the brakes from continuous heavy use
- B) Driving too slowly
- C) Brand-new brake pads
- D) Very cold weather

> **Answer: A** — Overheated pads and fluid lose stopping power — a major danger on long descents.

**Q24. The safest approach to a flooded section of road is to:**
- A) Follow trucks through at their speed
- B) Cross with the clutch fully pressed
- C) Avoid it if possible; if you must cross, go slowly in a low gear and test your brakes afterwards
- D) Speed through so the engine does not stall

> **Answer: C** — Water hides depth, washouts and open drains; wet brakes need gentle testing after crossing.

**Q25. An unsecured or protruding load on a vehicle:**
- A) Improves the vehicle's balance
- B) Is only illegal at night
- C) Is a serious hazard to other road users and an offence
- D) Is acceptable if tied loosely with rope

> **Answer: C** — Falling loads cause multi-vehicle crashes; loads must be secured and within legal dimensions.

**Q26. At night or in heavy rain, the two-second following rule should be extended to at least:**
- A) It should be reduced
- B) One second
- C) Four seconds
- D) Half a second

> **Answer: C** — Lower visibility and grip demand a much bigger safety gap.

**Q27. A pedestrian suddenly steps into your path. The safest reaction is usually to:**
- A) Accelerate past before they cross
- B) Swerve into the oncoming lane
- C) Brake firmly while keeping control and staying in your lane where possible
- D) Sound the horn and maintain speed

> **Answer: C** — Controlled firm braking sheds the most speed; swerving into traffic often trades one crash for a worse one.

**Q28. FRSC requires vehicles to carry which safety items?**
- A) An umbrella and torch only
- B) A television and loudspeakers
- C) A fire extinguisher and a warning triangle (C-caution)
- D) A spare number plate

> **Answer: C** — The extinguisher and C-caution are mandatory equipment checked at FRSC inspections.

**Q29. Brief 'microsleeps' while driving are a sign of:**
- A) Low fuel level
- B) The air-conditioner being too strong
- C) Good, relaxed concentration
- D) Dangerous fatigue — stop and rest immediately

> **Answer: D** — A 3-second microsleep at 100 km/h means over 80 metres travelled blind.

**Q30. After long, fast expressway driving, when you enter a town your speed often:**
- A) No longer matters in built-up areas
- B) Cannot be measured accurately
- C) Feels slower than it really is — check your speedometer
- D) Feels faster than it really is

> **Answer: C** — Speed adaptation tricks your senses; trust the speedometer and drop to the 50 km/h limit.
