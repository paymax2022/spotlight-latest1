# PART 1 — RESEARCH DOSSIER

## 1.1 Company history (HIGH confidence — Wikipedia, TechCrunch, TechCabal corroborate)
Founded 2014–2015 in Lagos by **Anton Volianskyi** (CEO) and **Vladimir Mnogoletniy**, incubated under **Genesis Technology Partners**, a Ukrainian tech-holding group with a track record of cloning classifieds/marketplace models into emerging markets. Positioned explicitly as "eBay + Craigslist for Africa." Registered corporate entity now domiciled in Gibraltar (Jiji.ng Online Marketplace Nigeria Limited). ~711 employees per PitchBook (2026).

**Acquisition-led growth is the core strategic pattern** — Jiji's own leadership describes the playbook as "enter a market, compete with the biggest local player, then decide whether to out-build or acquire":
- **2019:** Acquired OLX Africa's operations in Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, Uganda, Tanzania (Naspers/OLX had pulled out of Nigeria in Feb 2018) — this single deal removed Jiji's largest rival and roughly doubled its user base to 8M+ MAU.
- **2021:** Acquired Cars45 (used-car marketplace) — first move down-funnel from pure classifieds into inspection/trade-in services.
- **2022:** Acquired Tonaton, its main Ghanaian competitor; launched in Senegal and Côte d'Ivoire.
- **2023–2026:** Expanded outside Africa entirely — Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Pakistan (as "Milay"), and in **May 2026 acquired Bikroy** (Bangladesh's #1 classifieds site, also a Saltside Technologies property — the second Saltside asset Jiji has bought in four years after Tonaton). October 2025: launched Jiji.cg (Republic of Congo).
- Funding: **$50M total raised** across rounds including a **$21M Series C in Dec 2019** led by Knuru Capital, with TA Ventures, All Rise Capital, Kepple Africa Ventures, Flyer One Ventures, Somersault Ventures. Company states it reached **profitability** and is reportedly exploring a late-stage round or IPO within 24–36 months (MEDIUM confidence, single blog source on IPO timeline).

## 1.2 Scale and traffic (MIXED confidence — flagging conflicts explicitly)
- Company-claimed GMV: **>$10B annually by 2023**, **>$50B across seven markets by 2024** (LOW-MEDIUM confidence — single tertiary source, no primary filing; treat as a directional "very large" signal, not a hard number).
- MAU: 8M (2019, post-OLX deal, TechCrunch-sourced) → 15M+ claimed across 5 African countries by 2024 (MEDIUM confidence) → 12M claimed at Bangladesh-expansion announcement (2025, TechCabal-sourced, HIGH confidence for that specific figure).
- **Current traffic reality check (Similarweb/Semrush, April–May 2026, HIGH confidence, directly measured):** jiji.ng gets **~3.09M monthly visits**, ranks **#54 in Nigeria** (down from a widely-cited "#42" figure from 2024) and **#16,305 globally**. This is a meaningful signal: independently-measured *web* traffic is materially smaller than the company's user-base claims, which likely reflect app-only sessions, cross-market aggregation, or a real decline in web discovery as search and social shift the funnel. **Take the "15M+ MAU / 3M+ listings" company claims as upper-bound marketing figures, not verified metrics.**
- Traffic mix: **47% organic search** is the top channel (desktop); **WhatsApp Webapp is the #1 social referrer**, ahead of YouTube and Facebook — confirming the audit's D2 finding that deals migrate to WhatsApp constantly, to the point that WhatsApp is now structurally part of Jiji's own discovery funnel, not just its leakage point. Audience skews male (67%), age 25–34 dominant.
- Top competitor-by-audience-overlap per Semrush: **jumia.com.ng, konga.com, amazon.com** — i.e., Jiji is now measured against full e-commerce platforms, not just classifieds peers.

## 1.3 Monetization (MIXED confidence)
Confirmed structure (HIGH confidence, multiple independent sources agree): **listings are free forever**; monetization is 100% seller-side premium services:
- **TOP Ads** — 7–30 day placement boosts. Real regional pricing point found: **from ~KES 450/week in Kenya** (~$3.50) — i.e., deliberately priced for micro-entrepreneurs, not businesses.
- **Boost Packages** — 1–12 month tiers, described elsewhere as "Basic → Diamond" tiers.
- **Pro Sales** — cost-per-click auto-bidding across search/category/similar-ads placements (no upfront cost, CPC-only).
- **Jiji for Business** — a subscription CRM/analytics dashboard layer for high-volume dealers, aimed at raising ARPU from professional sellers specifically.
- Secondary: **Google AdSense / direct display ads** on-site, and **lead-generation commissions** from bank/insurance partners in the Cars and Property verticals.
- **Revenue-mix percentages (e.g. "65% from subscriptions, 20% from ads, 15% from commissions")** appear across several SEO-template "business model canvas" sites — **LOW confidence**: Jiji is private, doesn't publish financials (explicitly confirmed by ProductMint/PitchBook), and these specific percentages cannot be traced to any primary source. Treat only the *categories* of revenue as real; treat the *percentages* as invented plausible-sounding filler.
- **Independently confirmed drawback:** a 2020 hands-on test of Jiji's own Top Ad product (Kenya) generated 45 impressions, 1 phone-number view, and one cold chat lead over a week — versus a $0.30 Google Search ad producing two hot leads within three hours. This is a rare *outside, empirical* test of Jiji's core paid product, and it performed poorly — directly corroborating the user complaints about paid-boost ROI in the original teardown.

## 1.4 The trust/safety gap — current-state confirmation (HIGH confidence, and this is the headline finding)
**As of Jiji's own live safety page in 2026**, the platform's official fraud-prevention advice is unchanged from years ago: *"Avoid sending prepayments... meet in a public place... pay only after collecting the item... never give out financial information."* **Jiji still has no native escrow or payment-holding product built into the core transaction flow**, years after the drawbacks were first documented. The platform's entire trust model remains: free-text reviews + "check feedback" + user vigilance.
This gap is now visibly being filled by **third parties**, which is the single strongest piece of market validation for this PRD:
- **EscrowLock** — a standalone Nigerian escrow service that markets *directly against Jiji meetup risk*, citing a documented armed-robbery case where a seller was robbed at gunpoint by buyers who connected via Jiji.ng (Igando, Lagos). EscrowLock holds funds in CBN-regulated, NDIC-insured commercial bank accounts under its own corporate name and releases on buyer confirmation — i.e., it is running the exact escrow flow this PRD proposes, as a bolt-on, because Jiji won't build it natively.
- **"Marketplace Naija" / other new entrants** are positioning explicitly as the trust-native alternative to WhatsApp/Jiji social commerce, again centered on escrow.
- **Fake "Jiji escrow" phishing sites** exist and are documented as a scam vector across multiple African Jiji markets — criminals impersonating a buyer-protection product that doesn't officially exist is the clearest possible signal of unmet demand.
- **Regulatory framing (HIGH confidence, current as of March 2026):** the **CBN has not issued a specific "escrow license"** category. Escrow-type fund-holding in Nigeria currently operates under the existing **Payment Service Provider (PSP)** licensing framework, with funds required to sit in accounts at CBN-regulated, NDIC-insured commercial banks, and KYC via BVN/NIN expected of any compliant operator. **This is directly actionable for Paymax:** the marketplace's escrow sub-accounts should be built and represented as operating under Paymax's existing PSP/wallet licensing posture (via the Maplerad rail), not as a novel licensed product — this is both the compliant path and the honest one, and it should be stated exactly this way in any regulatory or investor conversation.

## 1.5 Market sizing (MEDIUM-HIGH confidence — figures vary by methodology but converge directionally)
Estimates for Nigeria's total e-commerce market cluster in a wide but consistent range: **$8.5–10.5B in 2024/2025**, growing at **~11–15% CAGR** to roughly **$18–23B by 2031/2032** depending on the research house (VMR, Mordor Intelligence, DataInsights, ResearchAndMarkets all land in this band; PCMI's methodology runs higher at $15B in 2023 → $33B projected by 2026). Consistent structural facts across all sources: **B2C is ~86–87% of the market**; **mobile accounts for 82%+ of online orders**; **C2C/classifieds is explicitly called out as the fastest-growing sub-segment continent-wide at ~19.8% CAGR**, still small in absolute base but structurally favored by three trends this PRD should lean into: rising digital literacy lowering trust barriers to P2P; **BNPL demand growing at 28.4% CAGR**; and **digital wallet share of transaction value projected to hit 22% by 2027** — i.e., the market is moving toward exactly the wallet-native, credit-adjacent commerce model Paymax is positioned to own, at the same time classifieds is the fastest-growing category. This is a genuine tailwind, not just a competitive argument.

## 1.6 Competitive landscape — the whitespace is bigger than "beat Jiji" (HIGH confidence)
The critical strategic finding: **no direct classifieds competitor in Nigeria has solved the escrow/trust gap either.**
- **Olist.ng** — structurally identical to Jiji (free classified ads, no payment facilitation) and is explicitly criticized in the same terms: *"Since Olist does not handle transactions, there is a risk of fraud or scams."*
- **Facebook Marketplace** — large reach via existing social graph, but zero platform-level payment or delivery infrastructure; arguably worse than Jiji on structured trust signals (no seller verification tiers, no ratings architecture).
- **Jumia / Konga** — real trust and logistics infrastructure (KongaPay + Kxpress in-house delivery) but they are **B2C-first inventory-owning or managed-marketplace models**, not P2P classifieds; they don't compete for the "sell my old phone / find a 2-bedroom flat / hire someone locally" use case that defines the classifieds category. Their existence validates that Nigerian consumers *will* trust wallet-based checkout and platform-managed delivery when it's offered — Jiji's category has simply never offered it.
- **Cars45 is now Jiji's own subsidiary** — meaning Jiji itself has already validated, with its own capital, that "classifieds + owned inspection/verification/transaction layer" is the correct evolution path for a single vertical (autos). **Paymax's opportunity is to apply that same logic — verified, transaction-completing infrastructure — across every category simultaneously, natively, from day one, rather than category-by-category acquisition years apart.**
- Niche players (PropertyPro, Cheki, 9jalist, HoBeei, Afribaba, Carmart) fragment specific categories but none combine cross-category breadth with escrow.

**Strategic conclusion:** this is not a two-horse race against Jiji. It is a category-wide structural gap — every P2P classifieds player in Nigeria, including the market leader, still runs on "meet a stranger, hope for the best." The first mover with **wallet-native escrow, delivered by an already-licensed, already-trusted fintech**, is not just taking Jiji's users — it is legitimizing an entire transaction pattern nobody in the category has been able to offer.

# PART 2 — WHAT CHANGES IN THE PRD (v1 → v2)

The architecture, domain model, state machines, API surface, ES/Redis design, mobile screen inventory, and admin console from **Paymax_Marketplace_PRD_v1.md** stand — nothing in this research invalidates the engineering plan; it validates the strategy underneath it. Five updates follow directly from the research above:

**Update 1 — Regulatory framing corrected (governs Pillar P1, Escrow).** Do not describe or pitch the escrow feature as requiring a new "escrow license" — none exists in Nigeria. Build and document it as a **PSP-framework fund-holding service**, with escrow sub-accounts resting in Paymax's existing licensed rails, KYC gated by BVN/NIN exactly as EscrowLock and comparable operators already do. This is both compliant and a faster path to launch than waiting on bespoke licensing.

**Update 2 — The competitive narrative widens.** Marketing, investor, and internal positioning should stop framing this as "a Jiji killer" and start framing it as **"the first trust-native classifieds infrastructure in Nigeria, full stop"** — Olist, Facebook Marketplace, and every other P2P player share the exact same gap. This is a stronger claim and it's the one the evidence actually supports.

**Update 3 — Monetization benchmarking gets real numbers.** Set introductory boost pricing with Jiji's actual regional entry point in mind (~$3–5 equivalent per week) as the *ceiling* for a comparable low-tier boost — Paymax's edge isn't cheaper boosts, it's that boosts stop being the *only* revenue lever once escrow fees (1.5–2.5%, per the original PRD) exist. Internally model the north-star metric knowing that even a modest independent test of Jiji's own paid product produced almost no conversion — meaning ranking-by-quality-signal (P4 in the original PRD) is not just fairer, it likely converts better than the incumbent's paid-boost mechanic, and that's a testable, marketable claim.

**Update 4 — Cars45 is a precedent to cite, not just a competitor to note.** Jiji's own $verified-by-its-own-capital decision to acquire a dedicated inspection/verification layer for automotive proves the thesis of Pillar P8 (category-specific trust products). Use this internally to pre-empt the objection "why would a classifieds platform need this much trust infrastructure" — the market leader already spent real money answering that question for one category. Paymax's plan is to ship that answer for every category from week one.

**Update 5 — WhatsApp is not just a leak to plug, it's a channel to design for.** Since WhatsApp is measurably Jiji's #1 external referrer already, the in-app deal room (P3) should include a **"Continue safely"** pattern rather than a pure block: if a user does leave for WhatsApp, a follow-up notification/deep-link should invite them back to fund the deal in escrow before it completes — meeting the platform's actual existing user behavior instead of only trying to prevent it.

**No changes needed** to: the domain model, FSMs, database schema, API surface, Elasticsearch/Redis architecture, the 33-screen mobile inventory, or the 8 admin modules from v1 — all remain the build spec. This document is the strategic and regulatory layer that sits on top of that engineering plan.
