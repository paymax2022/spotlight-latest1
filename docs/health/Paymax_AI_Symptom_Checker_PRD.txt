0.  Strategy in one page
An AI symptom checker is the cheapest, highest-frequency front door into healthcare — and for Paymax it is the front door to the entire care loop (Vet, Pharmacy, Lab, telemedicine). The global market is mature but built for Western patients on smartphones. None of the leaders are built for how Nigerians actually get sick, speak, connect, or pay. That gap is the whole opportunity.

The only symptom checker built for Nigeria/Africa — it triages in your language by voice on any phone (app, WhatsApp, USSD), understands local disease (malaria, typhoid, sickle cell, TB, maternal), and ends not in a frightening list of diseases but in affordable care you pay for from your Paymax wallet, with results saved in one place.
Why Paymax wins where Ada/Infermedica/Google can&apos;t: they stop at advice; Paymax connects triage to real, affordable, local care + payments + fulfilment + records in one loop — on the rails (wallet, KYC, MapService, last-mile, pharmacy/lab) it already owns.
Build posture: license a clinically-validated triage engine (don&apos;t build diagnosis from scratch), wrap it in an Africa-tuning + vernacular-voice + omnichannel layer, and scope strictly to triage & navigation, not diagnosis (safer, faster to market, avoids medical-device classification).
1.  Global competitive teardown
The serious players cluster into three archetypes: consumer apps (Ada, Ubie, Buoy, K Health), API engines you license (Infermedica/Symptomate), and LLM/enterprise efforts (ChatGPT, Microsoft Healthcare Agent, Google AMIE). Accuracy and — more importantly — triage safety vary widely.

Patterns that matter
Triage safety ≠ diagnostic accuracy. The leaders optimise to not miss emergencies (sensitivity), accepting over-referral. A 2022 systematic review found triage accuracy ranging widely (~49–90%). Emergency sensitivity is the metric that matters most.
Probabilistic engines beat pure LLMs on safety. Bayesian/inference engines (Ada, Infermedica) are auditable and safer at triage; pure LLMs diagnose well but triage unsafely. The winning pattern is engine for reasoning + LLM only for language understanding.
“Buy the engine” is the norm. Microsoft and many health systems license Infermedica rather than build a diagnostic engine — faster, safer, regulator-friendly.
Free triage → paid care is the business model. Ubie’s funnel (free checker → paid consult/Q&A) is exactly the model Paymax can run, but with a deeper care loop.
Everyone stops at advice. No global player closes the loop to affordable local care + payment + fulfilment + records. That is the open lane.
2.  The Africa gap — where every global player is weak
Africa-relevant efforts prove the demand and the channels, but none combine them with a super-app care loop:
Wellvis (Nigeria): COVID triage in English, French, Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, via web and USSD — proof that multilingual, feature-phone triage works locally.
Healthtracka / Lola (Nigeria): WhatsApp women’s-health AI + at-home phlebotomy (40,000+ tests) routing to a doctor — closest analogue, and a benchmark adjacent to Paymax’s Lab vertical.
Rwanda (Babyl-style): AI triage across ~2.6M consultations via USSD/SMS/web/app — proof of omnichannel scale.

3.  The competitive-edge thesis
Six differentiation pillars — each defensible because it rides rails Paymax already owns:
Africa-tuned clinical engine — local disease burden, drug availability, and care pathways, with malaria/typhoid/sickle-cell as first-class.
Vernacular + voice + low-literacy — Pidgin/Hausa/Yoruba/Igbo, voice-first input, body-map symptom picker.
Omnichannel reach — app, WhatsApp, USSD/SMS, agent-assisted; works on feature phones, low bandwidth, offline.
Care-loop + payments (the moat) — triage routes to a Paymax pharmacist consult, home lab test, telemedicine, or emergency, paid in-wallet, results to the vault.
Trust & safety — clinician-validated content, deterministic red-flag escalation, transparent “not a diagnosis,” human hand-off.
Cost & inclusion — free triage as acquisition; monetise the downstream care actions (the Ubie funnel, deeper).

4.  PRD — objective, personas, scope
Objective.  Give every Paymax user a free, fast, safe AI triage that works in their language on any phone, and route them to the right affordable care in the Paymax care loop — increasing health access while driving care-loop conversion. Scope: triage & navigation, not diagnosis.
Primary personas
The worried smartphone user (Lagos/Abuja) — wants quick reassurance and a clear next step.
The feature-phone / low-literacy user — needs USSD/voice/Pidgin; cannot use an English app.
The caregiver — checking for a child or elderly parent (family profiles).
The chronically ill (hypertension, diabetes, sickle cell) — recurring checks + follow-up.
The mother / maternal user — pregnancy/neonatal concerns needing extra-cautious triage.
Key user stories
As a user, I describe my symptoms by voice in Pidgin and get a clear, plain-language urgency level and next step in under 3 minutes.
As a feature-phone user, I dial a USSD code and get triaged without internet.
As a triaged user, I tap once to book the recommended pharmacist consult / lab test / doctor and pay from my wallet.
As a user in an emergency, I’m told immediately to seek emergency care, shown the nearest facility, and given first-aid steps.

10.  Data model & state machines
Entities:  Profile (self/child/dependant) · TriageSession · SymptomIntake · Evidence (symptom/risk-factor/answer) · Assessment · Condition · TriageDisposition · RedFlagEvent · CareReferral · EscalationCase · ConsentRecord · SessionReport · FeedbackRecord · ClinicalContentItem · RedFlagRule · LanguagePack · AuditEntry.
State machines (guarded transitions; side effects atomic; audit every transition)
TriageSession:  STARTED → CONSENTED → INTERVIEWING → (RED_FLAG_DETECTED → ESCALATED) → ASSESSED → DISPOSITION_GIVEN → REFERRED → CLOSED | ABANDONED
CareReferral:  CREATED → ROUTED(pharmacy|lab|telemed|emergency) → PAID → FULFILLED → FOLLOW_UP → CLOSED
EscalationCase:  RAISED → NOTIFIED(patient + clinician) → ACKNOWLEDGED → RESOLVED
ClinicalContentItem / RedFlagRule:  DRAFT → CLINICAL_REVIEW → APPROVED → PUBLISHED → DEPRECATED  (clinician sign-off required to publish)
11.  Safety & compliance invariants (SC-1…SC-12)
Symptom-checking is clinically high-risk. These extend the base HL-/NL- invariants and are release blockers.
SC-1 — Never present output as a diagnosis. Always “possible causes / guidance.” Scope = triage + navigation only.
SC-2 — A deterministic RED-FLAG layer can ALWAYS override the model toward higher urgency. Emergency detection is rules-based, never probability-only.
SC-3 — Conservative triage: on ambiguity, favour safety (over-refer). Optimise EMERGENCY SENSITIVITY first.
SC-4 — No prescribing, dosing, or definitive diagnosis by the AI. Prescriptions only via licensed clinicians in the care loop (HL-1/HL-3).
SC-5 — Human-in-the-loop for high-risk dispositions; clear hand-off to licensed humans; emergencies route to in-person care + ambulance + first-aid.
SC-6 — Clinical content + red-flag rules require licensed-clinician review & sign-off before publish; versioned & auditable.
SC-7 — NDPA 2023: explicit consent, sensitive-data encryption, access logging, data residency, right-to-erasure; minimise PII (engine runs de-identified where possible).
SC-8 — Medical disclaimer + one-tap emergency shortcut on every screen and every channel.
SC-9 — Extra caution for paediatric & maternal flows; child health routes to a caregiver and appropriate care.
SC-10 — No fabrication: the LLM layer only extracts structured evidence / serves curated content (RAG); clinical reasoning stays in the validated engine.
SC-11 — Regulatory posture: positioned as triage/navigation guidance, not a diagnostic medical device; monitor NAFDAC/SaMD developments; maintain clinician oversight.
SC-12 — Immutable audit of every session disposition, escalation, and content/rule change.

Success metrics
Emergency sensitivity (missed-emergency rate → ~0) · over-/under-triage rate · completion rate · time-to-disposition · care-loop conversion (triage→paid care) · language coverage & cross-language accuracy parity · follow-up engagement · retention · cost-per-triage vs revenue-per-converted-user.
Phased roadmap
Phase 1 (MVP): App + WhatsApp triage in English + Pidgin; licensed engine + red-flag layer; 5-level disposition; routing to Pharmacy/Lab/telemedicine with wallet payment; records vault; emergency screen. Clinician-validated on an African vignette set.
Phase 2: Voice (ASR) + Hausa/Yoruba/Igbo; USSD/SMS; body-map; Africa-tuning content depth; follow-up check-ins; epidemiology dashboard.
Phase 3: Agent-assisted; offline path; chronic-care journeys; outbreak-signal / public-health partnerships (ONSA/NDLEA/NCDC-style); deeper personalization from records.
Risks & mitigations
Clinical liability / unsafe triage → deterministic red-flag layer, conservative bias, clinician sign-off, shadow-mode validation, “not a diagnosis” scope.
LLM hallucination → LLM confined to evidence extraction + curated RAG; reasoning in the validated engine.
Regulatory (medical-device) → scope to triage/navigation, not diagnosis; monitor NAFDAC/SaMD; clinician oversight; legal review.
Trust / adoption → local clinician branding, vernacular, free triage, visible safety; agent-assisted onboarding.
Connectivity / inclusion → USSD/SMS/offline path; low-bandwidth design.
Data privacy (NDPA) → consent, encryption, residency, de-identified engine calls, access logs.
